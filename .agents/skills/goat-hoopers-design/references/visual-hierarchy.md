# Visual hierarchy review

Use for composition, density and color work. Read DESIGN.md's decisions,
composition, typography and semantic-system sections; those hold the values,
approved assets and exceptions. Start from actual content at the affected
revision, not a fresh mood board.

## Review through the surface's job

Trace one real task: finding this week's matchup, sorting a team, inspecting a
price, opening a full story or reaching Play. Identify the first actionable
element and the information that makes it useful. Compare the opening and one
populated working state at wide and narrow sizes, using identical content and
asset crops. Empty-only fixtures cannot establish directory or inspector
usability. Missing editorial and missing live data are separate cases.

When a layout feels heavy, inventory repeated headings, competing actions,
decorative boundaries and portrait space before adding a new component. Align
names, values and units in meaningful rows; use type and spacing to establish
the lead and its subordinate items. Keep the approved dense exchange useful.
An independent object may need a card; a sentence rarely needs its own box.

Map each proposed token to a semantic job and existing consumer. Check the
rendered foreground and composited background in the states affected by the
change; keep the pair, method and measured ratio with the evidence. Do not
infer contrast from a token name, a swatch or an opaque-color calculation when
the component renders transparency or imagery.

Finish with the five-field surface contract in DESIGN.md and a short before/
after rationale: what became easier to find or do, what data remained, and
which checks still need rendering. A card reduction that loses roster fields,
source periods or real navigation has failed the task.
