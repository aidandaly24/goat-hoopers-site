## Reproduced defect and targeted rechecks

**07: keyboard focus is lost when Undo disables itself.** At 1440×900 and 390×844, load member default, activate `#score-wall [data-compare]`, focus `#undo` and press Enter. The two lockers close and Undo becomes disabled; `document.activeElement` becomes `BODY`. Pointer activation reproduces the same result. Expected: focus lands on an enabled logical room action, ideally the comparison origin. Remedy: restore a surviving enabled origin after reaching the undo boundary, preserving scroll clearance. Exact reproduction and full hashes are in `art-c-evidence/repro07.json` and `defects.json`; `room.js` was `f8f75207345e13ae53b89804b10588829189ca4c231988391b74a76d55ad2b44`.

Root separately reported targeted repairs for 03’s focused boundary Previous control, 04’s replaced Next-game control and 19’s narrow Rank header. I paused inspection of those sources when they left the freeze. These are not claimed as independently originated findings here. 03 now moves focus to the enabled Next control at the Previous boundary; 04 restores the replacement Next game control; 19 uses a compact `#` label in the phone standings header. These were independently rechecked at both sizes and normal/reduced preferences; see `targeted-final.json` and the three per-study source hash records. The 07 repair and final all-twenty hash reconciliation remain pending.

No additional clipping or contrast defect was independently reproduced in the inspected paths. That statement is bounded; it is not an accessibility conformance claim. In particular, a disabled Back control and rendered room backgrounds cannot be assessed against an imagined text/background pair.

## Preserved #271 benchmark

I viewed `member-desktop-top.jpg`, `member-phone390-top.jpg`, public top images, and the complete member/public desktop and phone images only after the initial observations and pair judgments were written. The preserved capture source is `221bb52…`, retained research within the `64b9971` base, not current runtime or an approved style.

The benchmark gives the opening matchup, team desk and direct roster actions a familiar, compact hierarchy. Its GOAT logo, arena and real franchise faces supply recognizable league identity. Reading is consistent and the phone keeps the two opposing teams together. That is useful for a visitor whose first goal is to check the current pairing and open their team.

Across the complete page, the benchmark becomes a long ordered stack: roster, watch list, completed performers, news, editorial, saved slate, faces, directory and standings. The member phone image is 13,259px high. Repeated team/player summaries and many horizontal rules help scanning locally but make the whole site feel like one broad feed; reaching a later tool or directory can take substantial travel. The new ledger/search/court/branch/channel/planner studies offer more explicit ways to get to those tasks. Conversely, they ask users to learn a more specific navigation model, and several put the current fact or immediate team action later than the benchmark.

The benchmark’s muted paper/slate system is quieter and more familiar than the more assertive full-art directions. The new work has more distinct subject identities—court, fact wall, locker room, folio, yearbook, editable canvas—while varying readability and navigation cost. Neither the benchmark nor one new study is a universal winner across clarity, actions, league character and phone use.
