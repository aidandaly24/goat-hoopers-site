## Closest pairs, including grayscale

**09 / 15 is the closest broad content architecture.** Both place personal counted-player/roster work beside a long current-game/other-games field. The difference survives neutral geometry: 09 has a persistent navigation rail and an offset, narrower competition field that adds opponent and same-record-team reading; 15 has two near-equal fields, a lower start for the current game, fewer parallel team-context blocks and a roster summary contained in the left flow. Their normal first folds also differ in type scale, text measure and simultaneous task emphasis. No duplicate blocker. The gym drawing alone would not earn that conclusion. 09's long empty lower-left area is a concrete nonblocking cost of its unequal content lengths.

**05 / 06 share warm court-adjacent tone, but are not structurally interchangeable.** 05 opens with unequal player and standing readings, crosses a full-width game ribbon, then reverses proportions for tools versus the rest of the week. 06 opens with a ten-club index and then puts club/current game/opponent in three parts, followed by evidence and other games inside one continuous field. Their neutral complete pages preserve these differences. The green/yellow palette and court arcs are not the evidence.

**05 / 09 share player-list-first utility.** 05's large open names and right standing slice lead toward a broad game strip. 09's rail, contextual opponent field and matching-record clubs maintain a different reading frame through most of the page. Their list affordances are similar; their whole websites are not copies.

**06 / 11 share spatial league discovery.** 06 is an index plus framed court with a three-part current matchup; 11 is an ordered circuit surrounding the current game and then a normal multi-section scroll. The 11 circuit communicates supplied order, not a fabricated distance between clubs. This relationship is clear without color.

**03 / 14 share editorial reading, but differ beyond fonts.** 03 is an image-led two-column edition with a task/season rail and notebook; 14 is a continuous linked prose brief with marginal notes and very little imagery. Neither needs to become a dashboard to be useful.

**08 / 10 share analysis, but answer different questions.** 08 exposes individual completed-week observations through a wide points chart and player lookup. 10 groups current known modeled quotes by position, with selected named holdings and share proportions. Their chart/form proportions, home content and grayscale silhouettes differ. Both must retain their explicit period and coverage distinctions.

**01 / 12 share prominent owned-player imagery.** 01's photographic matchup and pulse lead into smaller portraits plus counted evidence; 12 opens with the unboxed group wall and places the matchup immediately beneath it. Their image/working-text distribution and lower-page organization differ. 02's new roll call further reduces the earlier 01/02 portrait-card convergence.

**13 / 17 / 20 share a calm document-like frame, not the same product.** Corrected 13 has date/subject/content columns with player observations and period-aware sequence. 17 has a left section index and player/opponent work followed by separately presented season records and games. 20 has a right route field and open task sections whose text/data proportions alternate. Those distinctions remain after neutralization.

**16 / 04 both offer selection, but serve different work.** 04 aligns two owned players against the same fact rows, while 16 collects selected public clubs around the member's own team and fixed opponent. Their initial page proportions and control relationships differ; the selector itself is not evidence of duplication.

**18 / 02 share Righteous display type, but not a skeleton.** 02's long manager roll call integrates an owned-player entry into a pairing row. 18 is an individual lookup/readout followed by a horizontal player digest and a bounded public league field. Neither palette nor the shared typeface is counted as structural variety.

**19 / 09 / 15 share two-sided fantasy context.** 19 separates evidence, current score, historical context and quote readings into staggered, unequal areas; 09 maintains continuous personal/competition fields beside a navigation rail; 15 maintains two nearly equal gym fields. The grayscale complete pages differ. 19's offset current scores are a usability tradeoff, not evidence that another whole-page architecture is required.

## Cross-set quality and remaining limits

The inspected member captures consistently identify the appropriate team, player set and opponent; guest captures supply real public league substance and account entry rather than an empty login wall. Missing versus zero and partial subtotal versus complete valuation are visibly retained. Most directions make the first three useful tasks some deliberate ordering of player inspection, owned-roster reading and current matchup/opponent/league context. These are valid shared manager needs; independence must come from their hierarchy and composition, not invented new tasks.

The compact supporting text is not uniformly as successful as the primary reading. 03's pair summaries/qualifiers, parts of 01's muted notes, and 10's legends/utility rows deserve scrutiny in the final parent review. The normal first folds across all twenty show varied working names, controls and data, not only different oversized headlines. There is no universal tiny-body pattern comparable to the rejected baseline.

Nonblocking refinement points: 09's unused lower personal-column space; 03's fixture-limited prose about following profiles rather than a strong ongoing editorial reason; 19's separated current scores; repeated free-throw/tools endings; and some small secondary labels. 16 intentionally exposes fewer initial facts than the denser directions, which is a product tradeoff for its club collection rather than a reason to demand a shared content template. These are concrete efficiency/content concerns, not numerical taste rankings. Retain all twenty directions for the parent comparison.
