# Concept 13 — The league, in time

Revised architecture checkpoint. Discovery only; no presentation source yet. Same author and same catch-up question. Own scope: `qa/homepage-personal-20/13/`.

## Two complete alternatives

**A. A chronological league ledger — selected.** Make the four supplied updates the start of a dated, continuous document. Every persona sees the league’s actual sample activity in time order; a member’s relevant entries have additional source context and local inspection actions. The document continues into the current league board, the genuinely saved 26 October model context, completed Week 2 player evidence, and clearly separated 2025 history. It combines useful updates, evidence and league access without making a roster table the homepage’s spine.

**B. A task index with dated evidence sections.** Begin with a compact list of member tasks — review the completed week, understand the current opponent, inspect the roster quote, locate the team in the league — with each action linking to a fully visible section below. Reaves would lead with completed-week evidence; NeuralNets with current-week context; guest with public pairings and standings. A wide single-column index would precede a narrow evidence reading column and a full-width league table. A complete footer would contain the real service destinations. This could work well for a visitor who already knows their task, but would require the member to choose a task before seeing the league’s supplied changes.

**Why A:** The question is about catching up on league changes and useful team evidence. Chronology lets a returning member scan what the source actually reports, then inspect its basis. It also gives guests a useful league document and keeps missing data as a qualification of particular facts. It does not turn missing-data repair into the league’s identity. There is no invented social activity, human post, transaction story, unread badge or “since your last visit.”

The earlier pale opening split and full-roster spine are superseded. The selected layout does not use a rail, club streams, a two-player contest, a court illustration, position allocation, a circuit, large athlete/editorial hero, or a matchup followed by three cards and standings. These are architecture constraints only; no peer source has been inspected.

## Selected geometry, navigation and imagery

Use a continuous, moderately dense document with full-width horizontal date rules, compact time columns and changing content widths. There are no independent card stacks. The page is bounded at approximately 1,200px. Each activity row has a short UTC time column, a compact team/subject column and a wider explanation/source-action column. A relevant member entry can continue into a full-width attached source strip under that same row. The strip is visually part of the entry, not a floating dashboard panel. At 1,120px the subject moves above the explanation within its row before text becomes cramped; timestamps remain associated with their entries. No fixed-height reader or nested vertical scroller.

The header is deliberately small: exact GOAT horizontal logo, Home marked current, an ordinary “Explore GOAT” disclosure for secondary real-site navigation, and the real account exit. A visible statement identifies those destinations as the current GOAT site. Directly below, the primary page navigation is a horizontal document index: Latest updates, League board, Saved context, Completed Week 2, History. These links jump to sections already in document flow. No content tabs or persistent side rail.

The page title is a practical date line, “Friday, 30 October / Week 3,” with a modest member context line or guest league introduction. “Synthetic demo” remains visible beside a labelled native demo-persona selector and an “About this data” disclosure. It is not a pretend production account switcher.

Use warm near-white ground, dark graphite text, restrained indigo links and fine muted rules. Alternate date-section grounds only where they aid location. No arena photograph, player hero, avatar wall or decorative imagery. The exact supplied logo supplies visual identity; a small player portrait is unnecessary in a source ledger. Structure mode neutralizes colors and images while preserving identical geometry/content.

1. **30 October: Latest updates.** All four supplied change entries appear, descending by their actual timestamps: NeuralNets quote coverage at 08:25, Reaves evidence at 08:20, NeuralNets matchup coverage at 08:15, Reaves quotes at 08:10. Local team inspections retain previous-season records and the NeuralNets previous-manager qualification. Link to History and Weekly archive on the existing service.
6. **Explore and semantic footer.** A compact set of task descriptions makes league tools and Arcade discoverable without claiming those tools function locally. Footer groups cover all fixture routes and repeat source/prototype context.

This is more than a sparse feed: four complete change entries, attached personalized evidence, five pairings, ten current standings, dated member player evidence or a guest directory, saved context where supplied, history, meaningful tool routes and complete navigation remain visible without opening an inspector. The full roster is not rendered as the main page spine.

## Typography and density

The revised roles replace the original Georgia/Verdana/Plex Mono scheme:

- **Reading and names:** `Tahoma, Geneva, sans-serif`, approximately 16px with 1.55 line height and a 65–70ch explanation measure. This is a direct working-page voice, suited to short update explanations and long names. Activity text may be 16px; table names stay at least 15px. No condensed face in prose.
- **Controls and navigation:** `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`, 14px regular/bold with clear visible labels, 40px minimum local targets and 44px principal actions. Native select/disclosures retain familiar keyboard behavior. Current-location styling uses shape/weight/text as well as color.
- **Data and times:** `Consolas, 'Liberation Mono', 'Courier New', monospace`, 14px/1.45 with tabular alignment. Use only for times, scores, positions/counts and quote values, never full names. Table rows begin around 40–44px and grow to contain wrapping text.
- **Date/section headings:** existing local licensed Lora at 600, approximately 30px for the practical date heading and 23px for section dates. This supplies hierarchy without a sports-poster scale. The existing font/OFL source stays unchanged; no font download.

Names such as Giannis Antetokounmpo, Stephon Castle’s Back and Kristaps Porziņģis wrap normally. Enlarged text grows rows and stacks subject/explanation regions. No shrunken text, ellipsis of important names, fixed-height clipping or scale-to-fit. No motion is required; any small focus emphasis must leave reduced-motion/still users all content and tasks.

## Local tasks, navigation and footer

The main task is change → supplied source context → return to the change. Hash anchors navigate to the visible Week 2 or board section and focus its heading. “Back to update” restores the originating element and browser Back restores meaningful history/position. Update text itself never pretends to submit an action.

Quote inspection uses a native disclosure directly under the relevant quote update. The summary already displays complete-versus-partial coverage. Open content contains the full 24/22 rows, labelled search, visible result count, a clear-search control and player-name inspection buttons. NeuralNets also has an unpriced-only control. These controls change local reading state only. Each player inspection prints identity, supplied position, quote state, evidence/period state and scope; closing returns focus to its trigger. Escape closes the active custom expansion when focus is inside. No hover-only action or separate inner scroll area.

Public team and pairing receipts expand at the board/directory row that owns them, with an explicit close/return control. They contain actual supplied identity, score state, current record and qualified history. For data not supplied to a public team, omit invented detail and offer a visibly external profile link. Important facts remain visible before opening.

Explore/footer destination groups: Home locally; League — Teams, The Table, Transactions, Draft; Evidence & tools — Stocks, Intel, AI Decides, Trade Analyzer; Stories & history — News, History, Weekly archive; Play — Arcade; Account — My Team, Sign in. Each destination that leaves the prototype is explicitly identified as the current GOAT site. The footer’s source explanation names the fixed synthetic as-of time, current-number construction, retained historical references and absent live/auth/backend connection.
