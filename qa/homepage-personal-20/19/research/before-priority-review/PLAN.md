## Two alternatives and selected plan

**A. Two uninterrupted opposing-team narratives — selected.** The homepage’s main geometry is an asymmetric pair of continuous articles: a broad viewer narrative beginning at the top left and a narrower opponent narrative beginning slightly lower at the right. They are both team narratives, never personal versus league columns. There is no shared metric matrix, section rail, repeating card scaffold, or score hero. The viewer article moves directly from team identity and season context into useful named player evidence and quote work. The opponent article follows its own shorter factual story: identity, current record/points, current score, dated history, and the boundary of available scouting evidence. Their independent lengths determine the composition; nothing is padded to fake a complete opponent report. A full-width “The rest of this week” section then opens the document to the league. A compact public history/Arcade tail and complete destination footer finish the website.

**B. One narrative with opponent annotations.** A single broad “Prepare for this week” document would combine the viewer’s available evidence into one continuous reading path. Opponent context would appear as short marginal annotations beside the corresponding current-score and season paragraphs; all other pairings would follow in the same document. This is usable and avoids a matchup application, but the fixture cannot support annotations beside player or quote evidence. That would leave uneven associations or imply comparisons the data cannot make. A is selected because its two separate team stories make unequal evidence honest and visually legible.

The selected architecture uses the opposition between two actual teams as structure. It does not use separate personal/league reading columns, a reference-book layout, linked brief, illustrated gym, court index, player wall, source ledger, allocation/circuit diagram, or score → three cards → standings sequence.
