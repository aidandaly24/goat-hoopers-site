## Three architecture sketches

These private sketches consider complete sites, not three hero treatments. No peer source or old prototype was inspected.

**A — A wide player wall.** A shallow brand/navigation strip; an almost full-width two-row owned-player wall with supplied points; a narrow matchup inset at the far right; a lower market/intelligence area; directory and footer. Guest replaces players with public team identities. Black/acid-yellow ground, condensed names and many small portraits create a wide, image-rich silhouette. Its first actions are player inspection, opening the team and matchup inspection. Rejected because few players have supplied portraits or completed-week points: this wall would give unavailable evidence visual equivalence and encourage repeated tiles.

**B — A court-side reading corridor.** A continuous arena-image column at roughly 40% width holds identity and ordinary navigation; a pale wide reading column holds personal notes, an opponent section and league context; a footer ends that column. Guest reads public summaries beside its league introduction. Georgia prose and a few large mono figures create a slow, photographic rhythm. Its first actions are evidence inspection, quote inspection and visiting the opponent. Rejected because the permanent photograph consumes useful first-fold space, and image-plus-stacked-sections does not earn enough of its image mass with work.

**C — Three parallel club streams, selected.** A compact full-width header sits above **three unequal, uninterrupted vertical color fields** with different reading rhythms and uneven image/text starts. The broad left field holds team identity and one focus player; the pale middle field carries player notes and supporting quote context; the dark right field holds the opponent and other games. All are visible together, sharing the browser's single document scroll. Their endings are staggered above one footer. The shape is a continuous three-column publication, not three framed dashboard cards.

This gives ownership, explanation and competition simultaneous presence without leading with a rich matchup hero. It changes the actual silhouette, navigation, image distribution and type mass. Full roster and full standings become useful local destinations rather than compulsory homepage sections.

The manager reconnects with their people and this week's opponent: **inspect a player, open their team, check the opponent**. Latest supplied changes explain evidence availability; they support those tasks rather than becoming a repair queue. There is no last-visit timestamp, previous quote, previous score or previous standing in the fixture. No personal since-visit delta, value trend or rank movement is claimed.

The focus player is a labelled editorial choice for this synthetic home, not a recommendation, performance ranking, starting-lineup claim or availability report. Reaves opens with Joel Embiid; NeuralNets opens with Giannis Antetokounmpo. Both have a supplied approved portrait and completed-week evidence, so a person and an inspectable fact can lead the page. ## Original visual world

Use a neighborhood basketball club after dark as the graphic world: **signal-orange team field, pale pistachio reading field, saturated ultramarine league field**, with black rules and the exact GOAT logo. Ordinary labels, navigation and details do the work. No ticket, jersey, noticeboard or room illustration substitutes for website controls.

The orange field has the greatest type mass: tall mixed-case team name, a modest transparent portrait at its approved 300px scale, selected player name and one large supplied points figure. The middle field is prose-shaped, with smaller headings and compact name/points lines. The blue field has off-white type, closely spaced paired-team names/scores and a small wide arena photograph placed lower in the column. The photo provides league context; no control or factual label is painted onto it. There is one focus player, not a trio of large owned-player cards.

No rounded repeated boxes, giant empty slogan, horizontal sequence of full-width bands or left application sidebar defines the page. Paragraphs, images, scores and links make each column's rhythm different. A shallow header and ordinary footer hold it together as a complete site.

## Complete homepage architecture

### Header

A pale header around 80px tall, with intrinsic growth, contains the exact black horizontal logo; Home marked current; local Team / Player notes / League anchors; and Stocks, News and Arcade exits. A focus-only skip link reaches main. The far end holds a labelled native **Demo persona** select and compact visible **Synthetic demo** label. The select changes `?persona=reaves|neural|guest`, defaults to Reaves and sets `document.documentElement.dataset.persona`. It is never described as real account switching.

### Broad left stream — Your team

Around 40% of the main width at 1440px. Show the actual team name, manager, supplied current record and standing. One portrait-led focus follows: Joel Embiid, C, **38 supplied Week 2 points**; or Giannis Antetokounmpo, PF, **45**. Include the completed-period label and the first inspection button. The portrait is contained at 300px maximum rather than stretched into a full-height image. These points are separate from the current Week 3 matchup.

**Open all your players** is a local navigation action below the focus, with roster count and a short description of the team detail. Quote context supports this destination: complete 24/24 coverage for Reaves; 16/22 with a labelled partial subtotal for NeuralNets. No unavailable-data task is promoted as a call to action. Two purposeful next destinations, Stocks and Trade Analyzer, end the column as labelled existing-site exits.

### Pale middle stream — Player notes

Around 34% of the width, starting slightly below the team identity to avoid a card-row alignment. Its introduction states the supplied period rather than inventing a performance story. All players with supplied completed-week evidence appear in ordinary flow as a short reading list, each with name, supplied points and a named inspection action.

Reaves: Damian Lillard 42.5, Tobias Harris 31, Joel Embiid 38, Myles Turner 24.5 and Derrick White 22. NeuralNets: Rudy Gobert 29.5, Giannis Antetokounmpo 45 and Kevin Durant 33.5. Other roster players remain available in the separate team view with explicit unavailable evidence.

Below, **Latest source notes** gives the two actual timestamped changes as modest explanatory text, with UTC dates. Reaves' new breakdown and complete quotes explain its evidence; NeuralNets' missing quotes and unreturned score explain reading limits. They get no red alert treatment, repair verb, fake refresh, “fix coverage” action or invented last-seen baseline. The shorter Neural notes leave room for a brief current-context paragraph derived only from its 0–2 record and papichooter's 2–0 record. It makes no coaching claim or prediction and does not borrow the previous manager's season as the current manager's record.

### Dark right stream — Around your game

Around 26% of width. Begin with a compact opponent/current-matchup block: correct opponent identity, both current records, supplied scores and explicit state. The third primary action opens local matchup detail. Its heading and image mass stay smaller than the team stream's, so the home reads as a personal club page rather than a matchup hero.

**The other games** contains the other four Week 3 pairings in unboxed compact rows. Full names wrap; scores and “Score unavailable” remain readable; each opens local detail. The member's own pairing is not duplicated. Synthetic in-progress state is labelled without a live clock or remaining-games count.

A small approved arena photograph then separates games from **League position**: the three supplied leading teams as a short ranked list and, for a member outside those three, their own standing. It is a current-standings excerpt, never a value rank or trend. **Open the full league table** reaches every standing/manager in a separate local view. A ten-row homepage table is not required.

Reaves' dated saved estimate belongs in the local matchup detail, reached by a clear **Saved model context** link beneath the current game. It gets no duplicate homepage probability graphic. NeuralNets gets a simple no-saved-estimate note in that context. No generation control is planned.

The blue field becomes **This week's games**, with all five public pairings, the top-three standings excerpt and the full-table action. These visible public contents replace personal content; the guest does not see empty member modules. Current synthetic season and retained 2025 information remain separate.

### Footer and destinations

One full-width semantic footer on a pale ground contains the exact logo, provenance disclosure, supplied as-of time and return-to-top. Group links by task: **League** — Teams, Transactions, Draft, Intel, The Table; **Players and decisions** — Stocks, AI Decides, Trade Analyzer; **Club** — News, History, Weekly archive, Arcade; **Account** — My Team, Sign in. Home stays in the header/logo. Every fixture destination remains discoverable without a giant header or every-feature body checklist.

Each real-site link has a visible external marker and nearby explanation that it leaves the prototype. Real `/teams/:id`, `/player/:id` and `/login` actions are labelled existing-site destinations. No fabricated article/transaction feed, historical value line, Lineup Power or fake login fills absent producers. Arcade has a clear direct destination; this concept does not require a large game feature.

## Local detail journeys and data truth

Primary controls use ordinary local navigation, preserving persona: `view=player&player=…`, `view=team`, `view=matchup&matchup=…`, and `view=league` query states. They render dedicated readable detail views, not one all-purpose modal or hidden tabbed homepage. Each has a named heading and **Back to your home** / **Back to league home**. Browser Back works, and return restores the initiating control and previous page position for the session without persistent storage. Player detail shows only supplied identity, position, portrait, completed-period points/state and current modeled quote/check time. ## Working typography and desktop behavior

- **Identity/headings:** existing local Barlow Condensed Regular, mixed case. Team name around 60px / 0.98, reducing to 48px at narrower desktop; section names 32–36px / 1.05. Its tall narrow shapes give the orange field athletic vertical mass. No synthetic bold from the single regular file. Team names wrap naturally rather than becoming a full-width poster line.
- **Reading:** `Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`, 17px / 1.5 in the pale field, 16px / 1.5 for support elsewhere; about 38–48 characters measure in the narrow reading column. Long explanations remain ordinary mixed-case prose, visibly distinct from display and controls.
- **Controls/data names:** `Verdana, Geneva, sans-serif`, 14px / 1.4 for navigation/buttons/labels, 15px / 1.4 for linked player/team names, weights 400/700. Full labels and visible keyboard focus. Giannis Antetokounmpo, Stephon Castle’s Back and Ware the Hoes At? wrap without ellipsis or smaller text.
- **Numbers:** existing local IBM Plex Mono Regular, tabular figures, 14–16px / 1.35 for quotes/records/dates, about 46px for the focus player's points and 26–30px for the quiet matchup score. The score is not the largest object. Units, sample period and unknown states remain beside the value.
- **Geometry:** about 1344px main width at 1440 with 48px outer margins and columns near 538/457/349px, including internal padding. At 1120 use 32px outer margins and columns near 390/350/316px. Everything has intrinsic height. If enlarged text makes three streams untenable, the league stream moves below the first two with all contents intact; text never shrinks to preserve a fold. This is desktop content-driven reflow, not phone design work.
- **Reading rhythm:** generous identity/portrait space left, short prose/name lines middle, compact paired-team rows right. Imagery and section starts are offset. Column backgrounds end with their content; no filler equalizes height. One document scrollbar controls all content. Frequent actions are immediate; optional feedback is brief/interruptible and reduced motion preserves everything.

Barlow Condensed and IBM Plex Mono are in the verified existing font catalog, with SIL OFL 1.1 notices, official source links and hashes. Reuse those bytes and preserve notices. Reading/control stacks need no download. No new fonts, dependencies, scripts or assets are planned.
