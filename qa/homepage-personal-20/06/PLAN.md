# Concept 06 — The court index

Same concept 06 author. The selected revised architecture is accepted for build. Own directory only: no peer inspection, shared helper, fixture edit, production change, dependency, git action or publication.

## Two alternatives and selection

**Selected: a continuous pale-hardwood court index.** All ten clubs form a useful two-row baseline at the top; the member club is identified in text. One continuous court field holds club/opponent facts and current score in its upper half, explicit inspection actions at the center line, then completed-week player samples and the other games together in its lower half. The court is the page's reading structure rather than a scene or a lineup simulation. Useful content remains semantic HTML, allowed to cover court rules wherever readability requires it. There is no sequential rivalry, player hero, then race composition.

**Alternative: a practice-led league homepage.** A real free-throw preview would sit beside the member's current pairing, with a compact club directory and player samples below. Its first task would be to enter the existing game. Rejected because the supplied scenario has richer member competition work than play context, and a practice-first homepage would subordinate that useful work to one external launch.

## Visual world, imagery and proportions

A broad maple-colored field, deep forest-green rules and ink, warm cream reading areas and small burnt-red action cues make an authentic basketball website. Proposed roles: maple #E9C58C, forest #173D35, cream #FFF6E5, red #A93928. Verify rendered contrast; court marks move behind opaque reading surfaces if needed.

At 1440px use about 56px gutters and a 1328px content width. At 1120px use about 28px gutters. Header about 72px; synthetic/persona utility about 42px. The ten-club baseline is two rows of five readable links, not a carousel. The upper court contains two club summaries around one compact score area. Center-line actions are ordinary links. The lower court pairs a complete short player-sample list with the other four games. These areas grow with content and enlarged text; no viewport-height scene, fixed-size canvas or internal reader.

Use the exact approved white GOAT logo on forest in the masthead. A shallow crop of the approved arena floor at the bottom of the field anchors the whole composition; scores and controls stay outside the image. Approved Embiid/Giannis portraits are small identity links within their actual sample rows, not giant athlete comparisons or invented lineup positions. The real free-throw preview gives Explore a concrete playable destination. All imagery is local and approved.

## Reading, controls and data

- **Archivo Black**, existing local file: short mixed-case team/section headings, about 25–30px/1.15. Longer names wrap; it is never paragraph text.
- **Barlow Condensed Regular**, existing local file: comfortably sized reading at 19–20px/1.45, roughly 45–58 characters per line. This is substantial text, not condensed microcopy. Small metadata uses the control face instead.
- **Space Grotesk**, existing local variable file: controls and names at 15–16px/1.4, weights 500–600; metadata at least 13px/1.45. Targets at least 44px tall, no identity ellipsis.
- **IBM Plex Mono**, existing local file: quantities at 15–16px with tabular alignment; the score is about 30–36px. Nulls are readable words in Space Grotesk, not compressed score numerals. Existing OFL notices remain available; no font downloads.

## Navigation and local tasks

Masthead: Home (current), club sheet/directory, games, Explore. A separate utility line visibly labels Synthetic demo, as-of time, Source notes and native Demo persona select. The persona query is never authentication. The baseline's ten club links open public local summaries. Semantic footer groups all existing fixture routes as Competition / Research / Other weeks and play / Account, visibly identified as current-site exits. Home returns locally.

First three member actions: inspect assigned matchup; open own team sheet; inspect one named completed-week player. Guest: inspect a pairing; inspect a club; open the local public directory. Each has a real fixture-only URL view, a heading, explicit Back to home and ordinary browser Back. Entry focuses the detail heading; Back restores origin focus/scroll where available. No modal, sliding reader or hidden primary homepage content. Team-sheet detail may use a dense roster table and optional labelled search; home does not.
