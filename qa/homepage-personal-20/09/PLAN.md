# Concept 09 — Your team, in company

Revised discovery plan. Await the early-three parent review gate and explicit build followup. Only this PLAN.md changes; there is no presentation code.

## Product discovery and the architectural choice

The useful question is “What are my players doing, and who does that put me alongside?” A ten-row standings board answers only the second half. This homepage will make a member's players useful immediately while introducing the managers across from them and the teams sharing their record. Current scores, prices and source limitations support those readings.

I privately sketched three complete website architectures before choosing:

1. **A league relationship map.** A horizontal identity/navigation header, large central relationship diagram, player evidence beneath its own team node, named opponent on the right, same-record teams on the left, then a public fixture index and tool/archive footer. The visual world would use a dark diagram field, large system labels, serif explanatory copy and mono numbers. Approved player portraits would sit only on the member branch. Team and player nodes would open local detail pages. This would be materially spatial, but the available fixture proves very few relationships: one pairing and shared records. Enlarging them into a diagram would spend too much space on names and invite unsupported rivalry or social-history claims. Rejected.

2. **A continuous clubhouse periodical.** A wide logo masthead and conventional horizontal primary navigation; one large personal lead story; two unequal reading columns for players and managers; a lower league fixture section; then Stocks, Arcade, archive and a broad site-map footer. Lora would carry prose, Oswald would distinguish short titles, and large numeric side notes would carry the facts. A shallow approved arena image would establish the publication's setting. Local story readers would hold the team and evidence detail. This would read well, but the fixture contains evidence rather than enough current editorial stories. It would drift toward a matchup opening followed by player features and a league band, a sequence the revised brief excludes. Rejected.

3. **A personal work column beside a column of league company — chosen.** A narrow full-height navigation rail sits beside two unequal, continuous content columns. The broad light column holds the member's identity, counted-player reading and useful roster entry. The narrower dark column begins slightly lower and holds the current opponent, named managers sharing the member's record, and the other games. Their contents run for different lengths; they are not cards locked to a common grid. The lower public game preview and reading destinations sit across the content area before a complete footer. The site feels like one inhabited club website, with substantial readable work in both columns.

The third architecture gives the whole website a recognizable silhouette and useful density without making a table the homepage. It also uses real fixture relationships rather than invented editorial hooks. Its distinction survives grayscale: rail, unequal columns, offset headings, portrait position, large player reading, narrow opponent dossier and different content lengths still define it.

The two columns share a reading relationship rather than a universal row scaffold:

- The personal column starts at the top. Its team name, manager and short current-season line lead into the counted-player reading. The score is not this column's title.
- The league column starts about 72px lower, so the two top headings have distinct roles. “Across from you this week” introduces the opponent as a managed team. “With your record” introduces two other managers through a specific shared fact.
- The personal portrait sits on the outer edge of the team introduction, not inside every player row. Player numbers remain adjacent to names.
- The other games continue down the league column while supporting roster context continues down the personal column. There is no page-wide competition block or three-player strip.
- At larger text settings, the columns may stack in source order when their minimum readable widths no longer fit. The navigation becomes normal-flow wrapping navigation before it can constrain the reading width. No essential text is reduced to preserve the silhouette.

## Local tasks, Back and source honesty

**Inspect player:** activate a clearly labelled player action. A supplemental inline evidence reading opens directly below that player, receives a stable hash and focus on its named heading. It shows only supplied counted points, synthetic Week 2 date bounds, participation meaning and modeled quote when known. It cannot invent individual game logs, a box-score receipt, injury or schedule. Close and browser Back restore the original player's action.

**Open roster:** navigate to a complete local view in the same prototype, retaining persona and a meaningful Home return. It shows team identity, all supplied roster references, prices/Unpriced, coverage and a labelled evidence column. The home already shows the useful available evidence, so this destination is a deeper task rather than hidden primary content. The six-quote-gap link lands on its named group. Closing/back returns to the exact home entry and scroll context.

**Inspect or compare team:** open a normal-flow local team reading from the opponent or same-record introduction. Both sides have manager, current record, points for, supplied rank and pairing. No value comparison appears unless complete comparable values actually exist, which they do not here. No inferred ranking formula, movement, rivalry history or playoff prediction is added.

**Inspect matchup:** show both identities, current synthetic score state and source time. m1 additionally has the saved 2026-10-26 08:00 UTC model estimate, returned Reaves choice, all 56%/44% probabilities, independent 35% confidence and the supplied not-calibrated-odds label. m4 keeps the real zero separate from unavailable and has no saved estimate. There is no model-generation or fake refresh action.

All local views retain identity, destinations, a visible Back/close and a sensible focus target. Supplemental nonmodal readings need no focus trap. Query/hash handling preserves the demo persona and supports direct links and Back/Forward. Press/focus feedback is immediate; no opening animation can lock out input. Reduced-motion users get the same complete reading and tasks. Nothing saves a lineup, submits a trade, logs in, creates a notification or calls a live API.
