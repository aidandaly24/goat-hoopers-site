# Concept 09 — Your season in the league

Discovery complete; awaiting the root's build followup. This is a complete desktop homepage proposal, not presentation code. Ownership is limited to `qa/homepage-personal-20/09/`.

## Discovery answer

A member should be able to locate their season among the ten people playing it. The front page will therefore lead with the complete league table, named managers, the viewer's position and their opponent's position. Beside that shared context, a personal reading column explains the next useful inspection. The score is supporting evidence within that relationship. The modeled quote belongs with the roster that produced it.

The page's main action is **inspect the evidence behind my position**, starting from either a named team or the member's newly available player evidence. It will not pretend that two completed weeks establish a season trajectory, playoff odds or a valuation rank. “You are first” is supplied context; “you will finish first” is not.

The obvious default I rejected is a very large own-team score and roster-value number above a row of interchangeable news cards. It would obscure the other managers, give a partial NeuralNets quote misleading prominence, and make the guest edition an advertisement rather than a useful league homepage.

## Whole-website architecture

The document uses a warm white open canvas, dark ink, a restrained rust action accent and quiet row rules. The exact black GOAT horizontal logo anchors the header. Team names, managers and player names supply identity throughout; a team is never reduced to a colored number. A small “You” label and a separate “This week's opponent” label accompany any row emphasis. Color is supplementary.

1. **Header and navigation.** Exact logo links to local Home. The main destinations are Home (current), Teams, Stocks, News and Arcade. Public GOAT exits carry visible “GOAT site” text and an accessible explanation that they leave this prototype. Member account access is one “My Team on GOAT” link; guest access is “Sign in on GOAT”. A separately labelled **Demo persona** native select offers Reaves, NeuralNets and Guest. “Synthetic demo” is always visible beside it and links to the source explanation. The selector controls only `?persona=` and the root `data-persona` attribute; default is Reaves.
2. **Opening context.** A modest mixed-case page title, 2026 / Week 3 and a short sentence establish the visitor's situation. No court-sized hero, ticker or promotional statement precedes the work.
3. **League and personal reading.** Approximately 60% of the working row is the ten-row semantic standings table: rank, team/manager, W–L, points for and explicit Inspect action. The remaining width is a normal-flow personal column. Reaves receives new evidence, current opponent context and a compact complete-quote notice. NeuralNets receives score availability, quote coverage and the named opponent. Guest receives the full five-pairing public list and account entry. There is no internal scrollbar. At 1120px the table drops the inspect text onto the identity cell's next line if needed, and manager names continue to wrap.
4. **Roster readings (members).** An open full-width roster table retains all 24 or 22 players. Columns separate name/position, counted Week 2 points, modeled FAAB quote and Inspect. The available evidence rows lead; the remaining rows clearly say evidence unavailable. NeuralNets additionally groups its six unpriced rows under an explicit subheading so the primary coverage action has a meaningful target. The other sixteen stay visible. A source line says that the counted sample does not reconstruct the team's full weekly score. Reading a quote does not claim participation; missing participation does not imply a DNP or injury.
5. **The rest of this week.** Members get all five pairings in a compact readable list, retaining the own matchup with its “Your matchup” label. Each side has a team name and manager, then the score or its availability state. Any pairing can be inspected locally. The saved estimate for m1 appears only in that pairing's expanded supporting evidence; no estimate is invented for the other four. Guest already has the pairings above and uses this region for the ten-team public directory's available historical context, avoiding a duplicate scoreboard.
6. **Between games.** Two modest useful areas connect reading to action: market inspection and trade analysis links, and a real free-throw preview with **Play on GOAT — leaves prototype**. The approved game preview is the only large-ish image, around 280px wide; it explains an actual playable destination. No fake local game or new image download is planned.
7. **Footer as a site map and trust reference.** Three groups: Follow the league (Teams, Transactions, The Table, Intel, Draft); Research and remember (News, Stocks, AI Decides, Trade Analyzer, History, Weekly archive); Visit GOAT (Home, Arcade, My Team, Sign in). All supplied destinations remain discoverable. The prototype's local Home is distinct from the labelled external GOAT Home. The visible source explanation names the synthetic scenario, 08:30 UTC as-of instant, authorized identity references, missing data and historical qualifications. It also states that the local selector is not account membership.

The footer does useful work rather than repeating an enormous header. Public team `/teams/:id` and player `/player/:id` links are offered from the corresponding local detail with the same visible exit label. A prototype inspection and the production destination are separate controls.

## Typography and density

The distinction is between reading people, operating the website and checking quantities.

| Role | Choice and concrete treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Page title and reading copy | System Georgia, with Times fallback. Title 32px/1.15 at ordinary desktop size, normal weight; paragraphs 17px/1.55, about 60–64 characters wide. The serif gives managers and explanatory sentences an unhurried human voice without adding a font payload. No all-caps body or stretched team headline. |
| Team/player names in rows | Georgia 16px/1.3, bold only for a selected identity. “Stephon Castle’s Back”, “Giannis Antetokounmpo” and “Kristaps Porziņģis” wrap naturally. Team and manager are separate lines. Minimum name column width is roughly 14em, never a forced single line. |
| Navigation, controls and section labels | `system-ui, Segoe UI, Arial, sans-serif`, 14–15px/1.3, 600 weight; section headings 20px/1.25 at 650 weight. Plain mixed case, clear button verbs, 44px target height. Manager/support labels are 14px/1.4. |
| Data | Existing local IBM Plex Mono Regular, 14px/1.4 with tabular numerals; headline score 28px/1.1 maximum. Right-align quantities, preserve explicit units, use stable two-decimal score/points presentation. Missing values are words, not numeric glyphs. Static regular font is not synthesized into a heavy data weight. |
| Sources | System sans 14px/1.45; complete source explanations remain readable. Compact visible labels never carry the sole essential qualification. |

Only the already licensed IBM Plex Mono file is proposed; its adjacent OFL notice remains intact. The approved catalog and the font's local OFL header were read. No DM Sans, downloaded font or new dependency is needed. Headings use slightly tighter tracking, body near zero. Rem-based spacing and intrinsic row heights let enlarged text grow. At 1440px the opening table and personal action should fit comfortably in the first working screen; at 1120px content wraps instead of shrinking the font. These are build targets, not verified render claims.
