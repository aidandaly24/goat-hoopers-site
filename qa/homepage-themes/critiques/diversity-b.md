# Independent visual diversity review — B

All 63 assigned pairs have actual visual evidence and a D or R classification. I found no near-duplicate pair within this assignment that calls for duplicate-led design rework. This is a qualitative pixel comparison, not a shortlist, taste score or formal approval. Concrete reasons for every pair are in the matrix below and in `pair-results-b.json`.

I read the protocol and pair assignment first, then formed judgments from images. I did not read authors’ DESIGN/allocation explanations, other critics’ findings, or design/source code. The common league content and subject matter did not count as distinctness; the judgments use whole-page composition, imagery/court treatment, typography, shape, density, atmosphere and value distribution.

## Coverage and limits

For themes 01–19 I viewed complete member desktop and phone images at overview scale, then opened member top, middle and bottom crops separately at their native 1440 px desktop / 390 px phone width. I also viewed complete public desktop and phone overviews and public top/middle/bottom checkpoint composites. The public desktop panels are reduced to 720 px, while the public phone panels retain their native 390 px width. The exact 230 image paths are recorded in `pair-results-b.json` under `viewed_files`.

I initially saw contact sheets for 01–13, then re-opened the final contact sheets for 01–20. All 80 full-page capture files existed at final inspection. Theme 20 has no B-assigned pair and was seen only in the final contact sheets; I make no individual-page or lower-page claim about it.

Tall full-page images establish composition and section rhythm, not a line-by-line audit. Native detail sampling covers top/middle/bottom rather than all intervening pixels. Public native 1440 px desktop crops were not opened separately. I did not test interaction, keyboard, focus, hover, opened disclosures/rosters, data correctness, source links or physical devices. Public/member detail beyond the listed files and sampled regions is unclaimed.
