## Related assigned pairs and bounded separation

- **01 / 04 — R.** Both are dark neon sports worlds. 01 uses condensed white headings, lime/lavender scores and a faint full court; 04 uses cyan/pink pixel headings, offset arcade frames, a game-led opening and a two-column team register. Those differences persist below the hero. Bounded separation: keep 04’s arcade frames and pixel typography in lower sections; do not turn either body into the other’s flat row system.
- **01 / 16 — R.** Both use tall condensed sports headings over a dark, ruled data page. 01 has a navy/lime/lavender court plan and repeated colored roster anchor bars; 16 uses a black/gold photo overlay, film-like angled opening edges and a narrow numbered body rail with gray portrait chips. Bounded separation: carry 16’s cinematic framing into its dated-edition and archive treatment so its restrained body retains a stronger identity.
- **02 / 19 — R.** Both are square, print-like grayscale sports registers with numbered sections. 02’s huge wide display type, alternating pure black/white sheets and large left indices differ from 19’s constant concrete-gray field, narrow condensed headings, red rules and black index boxes. Bounded separation: preserve the alternating slab composition in 02 and the continuous industrial ledger in 19; a palette swap alone would collapse that distinction.
- **05 / 16 — R.** The opening in both is an arena photo darkened behind a scoreboard, followed by restrained near-black ruled data. 05 uses wide futuristic display numerals and cyan/chrome highlights, keeps top values as a separate large section and places standings beside rosters. 16 uses tall condensed type, gold film edges, a combined seat/value block and numbered left rail; standings come after rosters. Bounded rework recommendation: strengthen 16’s cinema language in one editorial and one lower section; retain its distinct rail and avoid 05’s chrome display treatment.
- **06 / 14 — R.** Both have a warm dark court/club atmosphere with muted orange/brown rules and an arena still. 06 uses condensed type plus handwritten asides, an orange torn-edge score block and mostly continuous dark lower sections. 14 uses serif type throughout, a photo-left matchup-right opening and a prominent cream editorial band. Bounded separation: keep 06’s street-paper edge and handwriting and 14’s serif/light editorial interruption; do not flatten both into the same dark brown row page.
- **09 / 20 — R.** Both use friendly pastel fields, rounded controls and circular portrait treatments. 09 is a sky-blue cartoon playground with chunky display type, outlined court-map artwork, peach cards and a five-pick tile grid. 20 is a quieter white/mint/lavender page with slim sans type, low-contrast court lines, arch portraits and ruled AI rows. Bounded separation: retain 09’s illustrated outline system and 20’s quiet arch/row system; do not substitute the same rounded card grid into both.
- **15 / 20 — R.** Both use geometric sans type, rounded color fields and portraits against shaped backgrounds. 15 has saturated royal blue/yellow/coral, overlapping offset score shapes, bold wide headings and strong outline/shadow accents. 20 uses pastel mint/lavender/coral, quiet thin rules, pill controls and arch portraits; its score stays inside a soft white card. Bounded separation: keep 15’s overlap and saturated rhythm and 20’s quiet inset treatment through archives and team lists.
- **16 / 19 — R.** Both use tall condensed headings, restrained arena imagery and rule-separated sports data. 16 is black/gold with a full photo-overlay opening and left numbered rail; 19 is light concrete-gray with red vertical/horizontal dividers, boxed roster numbers and a separate anchor index after the register. Bounded separation: preserve those body structures and strengthen 16’s film framing beyond the hero so a future gray/gold palette change cannot erase the distinction.

No diversity replacement is required by my assigned-pair findings. The bounded suggestions above preserve differences already visible, with particular attention to 16’s lower-page cinema identity. Root still needs its combined-pair reconciliation; my R is not an assertion that a later recolor would remain distinct.
