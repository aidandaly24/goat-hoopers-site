The public front page answers “what is happening in this league?” Its first action is to inspect the five current pairings or explore the featured teams. A wide, natural-ratio arena image establishes the place; the current pairing sits beside it without text over the image. The dated editorial follows immediately, then the full league reference.

The member workspace answers “what should I look at for my team?” It starts with the manager’s team identity, standing, roster count, a working matchup desk and modeled roster total. My team and View roster are the first actions. A form notebook, completed-week evidence and team wire precede the shared reference. This is a distinct opening composition and reading order, rather than a personal card added above the public front page.

The original court is a league landmark in both editions: the public feature uses its full frame; the member version uses a small reference thumbnail. The full common inventory remains accessible: five saved AI pairs with independent confidence/returned choice/provenance, all ten faces, dated happenings, ten searchable and sortable full-roster disclosures, ten sortable standings rows, stats and Power unavailable states, synthetic transactions, Stocks/trade/Arcade paths, both dated archives, and editorial source notes. Recorded team-value history remains explicitly unavailable.

Relevant states are public/member, known-zero/pending/incomplete scores, dated draft/stale editorial, ready/stale/missing AI, unpriced roster values and unavailable history/Power/stat producers. The fixture warning is prominent and all dated sample opinions retain their period, source, selection basis and draft status. The current pair uses directory data. The old opener narrative is qualified inside source notes rather than presented as current opponent evidence. Ready AI splits use probabilities by roster ID; the explicit returned choice is rendered independently of the largest probability. Stale picks lose their split bar and are labelled visibly. Missing slate is unavailable.

At narrow widths, public court precedes its pairing; member matchup precedes value, notebook and wire. All shared content reflows into a single column except the compact ten-face index and completed-week comparison. Full rosters become one column. Menus, inputs and primary actions retain 44px targets; native disclosures and labelled controls support keyboard use. There is a focus-only skip link, visible focus, and Escape closes/restores focus to the navigation disclosure. No auto motion runs.

Forbidden patterns: fabricated current awards/history/rankings, inferred model choice, replacing null score with zero, text on busy imagery, repeated generic card grids, lost roster names on mobile, nested interactive controls and model generation on page render.

This is an offline HTML design fixture, not proof of Next.js routing, authentication, live data, game operation or physical device/screen-reader behavior. App destinations are ordinary source-route links; no backend is imported. No network data, installs, services, paid assets or production edits are used.
