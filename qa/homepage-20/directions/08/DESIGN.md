# Direction 08 — Clubhouse Noticeboard

The question is “What is on our league’s board, and what do I need to look at for my team?” The first action is a current matchup team link; members also get an explicit View roster action beside their own identity. The opening distinguishes the current synthetic Week 3 pairing from the stale October draft edition. The known home score is 0.0, while the missing away score stays Pending and scoring stays incomplete.

This is an open bulletin for ten friends: a large matchup lead and original court photograph balance an offset white clipboard. Below it, a lead portrait, a smaller criticism and a wide scouting notice make a deliberately asymmetric editorial pin-up. The wire sits alongside those stories. The saved AI slate reads as a numbered list, the ten faces as a contact sheet, and the full directory as a working roll call. Large Inter headings, small dated notes and aligned Geist Mono figures give each kind of information its own voice. Paper + Slate remains the shared semantic palette; the real arena and portraits supply the color. There are no torn-paper effects, new illustrations, decorative buttons, fake charts, dashboard-card grids or animation.

Members receive the supplied personal summary only: roster identity/count, standing, modeled FAAB total/rank/coverage/computation time, three priced players and all 24 values in native details. Form-based scouting, synthetic completed Week 2 performers, team news and recent moves remain distinct from price and older editorial judgments. Public visitors see the league notice and standings preview instead. Both views retain every league section.

Saved probabilities map to roster IDs rather than array order. The returned choice is displayed explicitly, even when it differs from the largest probability. Confidence is a separate field. All five ready pairs include saved time and expandable model, prompt, distribution and evidence provenance; unavailable input snapshots stay unavailable. `?ai=stale` marks every saved row visibly stale and removes the distribution bars, while preserving the old numeric probabilities with their stale context. `?ai=missing` shows Unavailable rather than invented values. Recorded value history, aggregate league stats and Lineup Power have explicit missing-source states.

The narrow layout stacks court, stale-edition context and clipboard before the rest of the board. Editorial notices become compact portrait-and-text rows; AI pairs keep both probabilities and the returned choice; the contact sheet uses two columns; directory and table fields wrap without removing data. The complete rosters remain native disclosures. The sticky menu provides all league destinations and public/member comparison links. Its clearance is measured from the actual rendered header. Escape closes the phone menu and restores focus to its summary. There are no modal controls.

Search checks team, manager and every roster name. Team ordering supports roster number, alphabetical name, roster size and PF. Standings ordering supports rank, name and PF in both directions. Missing values are never converted to zero. Previous-season NeuralNets attribution remains visible in the collapsed directory row and inside its roster. All 228 unique roster references link to their actual player routes.
