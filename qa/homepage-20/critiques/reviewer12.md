## 09 · Stats Exchange — advance for the working reference

**Why this belongs in the three.** The [desktop opening](../evidence/09-member-desktop-top.jpg) uses the available width to answer several actual questions without a large ornamental title: opponent and incomplete scores on the left; roster value, top players, coverage and two useful actions on the right; saved AI and observed form already beginning below. The full capture continues this same hierarchy through editorial, completed-week evidence, news, faces, directory, standings and archive. It feels like a place to return to for a specific lookup.

The [native desktop middle](../evidence/09-member-desktop-middle.jpg) is especially effective. FP and GP are aligned and distinct; the completed Week 2 period and small-cohort exclusions stay close to the selections. The dated happenings and current transactions occupy different named groups. The [directory/footer cut](../evidence/09-member-desktop-bottom.jpg) keeps records and opponents in useful columns rather than turning every team into a large card. The public opening supplies a real directory/market entry point instead of leaving an empty member panel.

**Meaningful costs.** The [phone opening](../evidence/09-member-phone-top.jpg) spends the entire first capture on chrome, page identity, chapter links and the featured matchup. My team/View roster are not visible there. Its helpful desktop density does not automatically make the mobile first action fast. The court becomes a small stamp beside explanatory text; “Our court, between games” has less purpose than #271's clear court-to-Play caption. The separate Across the league shortlist, Faces section and directory revisit some of the same identities/prices. The [public phone middle](../evidence/09-public-phone-middle.jpg) shows how quickly a two-column face grid becomes many short name/ownership/value lines with long identities such as Shai Gilgeous-Alexander.

**Iteration brief.** Preserve the compact tables, aligned figures and open desktop columns. Put My team and View roster in the phone cover before the explanatory court note; do not wait for the stacked valuation section. Give the bounded court a useful immediate game link/caption while keeping it small enough that this remains the dense reference alternative. Reduce repeated scene-setting and duplicate price-only appearances where they add no new basis; retain all ten faces and the complete directory. Keep source period, GP, pricing basis, returned AI choice and confidence explicit. Review the native phone result for long names and metadata hierarchy rather than recovering space through smaller type.

**Concrete next acceptance path.** On a phone: identify the opponent and incomplete scoring, activate View roster, return, search a long player name, then sort standings. On desktop: distinguish observed FP/G from modeled FAAB and completed-week totals without opening notes. Recheck that the saved choice can remain the 35% team even when the other team has 65%.

## 16 · Two editions — advance for meaningful visitor/member composition

**Why this belongs in the three.** This is the strongest distinction between the two audiences in the reviewed set. Compare the [public desktop top](../evidence/16-public-desktop-top.jpg) with the [member desktop top](../evidence/16-member-desktop-top.jpg). Public gets the league front page, a substantial original home court and concrete routes to all five pairings or teams. Member gets the actual team identity, matchup, roster valuation and a set of workspace anchors. This is a change of emphasis and order, not just removing a private total from an otherwise identical column.

The court remains owned league imagery with no overlaid prose. The public pairing sits beside it on desktop. Member standing `1/10` and value rank `2/10` have distinct labels. The [member middle](../evidence/16-member-desktop-middle.jpg) offers a legible directory/standings spread; the [public full capture](../evidence/16-public-desktop.jpg) keeps the shared reference and archives after the editorial opening. The dated stale draft is visible near the public title, rather than being discoverable only at the footer.

**Meaningful costs.** The member opening repeats Reaves Dropper in a very large identity headline and again in the matchup. At [phone size](../evidence/16-member-phone-top.jpg), the large heading, timestamp and two rows of workspace links consume much of the first screen before the matchup, while the paired roster action is lower still. The public phone places the large court before the matchup, so its first score is near the bottom of the top capture. Boxed `01`/`02` labels beside teams could be read as rank/seed unless their roster-ID meaning is stated. The [phone faces](../evidence/16-public-phone-middle.jpg) repeat a team link, player link, dollar value and “Price details” for each person; that is considerable vertical cost before a second team directory.

**Iteration brief.** Keep the two distinct openings and their shared complete reference. Compress the member title/date block and put both team actions beside the first identity; the header's My team link alone does not replace View roster. On public phones, bring the actual pair above the court or reduce/reorder the court so both teams and status arrive earlier. Label meaningful roster IDs, or remove the numbered box where it adds no task. Tighten the face catalogue's repeated action wording while keeping all links/data available. Reduce overlapping section titles such as the shared-reference eyebrow, “Five pairings. Ten sides.” and AI Decides when they describe the same block.

**Concrete next acceptance path.** Switch Public → Member → Public at the same width. Each view should make its different first task clear while preserving all ten teams/faces, five saved pairs and full roster references. Test both mobile openings with the longest names and enlarged text; confirm no member values, completed-week personal performers or personal wire remain in Public.

## Why the other three stay as reserves

**01 · Courtside Newspaper** has the clearest editorial voice. The [desktop top](../evidence/01-member-desktop-top.jpg) and three-take/sidebar arrangement in the full capture feel authored, and its public desk is useful. Its price is an oversized masthead/headline before data and heavy repeated rules. The [phone top](../evidence/01-member-phone-top.jpg) shows no member action before the court; “Week 3 is still on the floor” uses much of the screen to restate the incomplete state. The native directory cuts have generous space for a compact lookup task, and the fine-print section becomes another large headline/section. It would be my editorial reserve if one of the three concepts is dropped. I would shrink the opening/title overhead and bring member actions forward before changing its personality.

**02 · Coach's Clipboard** has a purposeful original-court treatment: the numbered diagram is visibly a navigation map, with matchup/scouting/roster destinations named below. Its saved slate and shared best/rough selection handling are orderly. But the [phone top](../evidence/02-member-phone-top.jpg) spends almost a screen on working-sheet title, fixture-week stamp, stale-edition strip and account band before the scores. The full page repeats strong section rules/numbers through twelve chapters, and the large face portraits take more space than their reference task needs. It is a coherent alternate voice, with more opening scaffolding than the three advances need.

**07 · Schedule Board** has a strong information decision: all five actual score pairings and their saved model output are together, and top modeled faces are integrated with directory teams. The selected pair is clearly Yours/Featured. However, the five-row slate precedes the member valuation/notebook; on phone it makes the personal desk a later destination. The [phone directory cut](../evidence/07-public-phone-middle.jpg) devotes most of a screen to one team's identity, opponent, portrait/price and roster action. The court/Arcade thumbnail supplies a destination, but less of the league's visual identity than 16 or 18. Preserve this integrated schedule as a useful reference pattern; shorten row and opening overhead before treating it as the full homepage winner.

## Comparison with #271 and evidence limits

The preserved #271 opening already has a valuable baseline: recognizable identity, two plainly readable teams, a tight court crop and a direct Play free throw link, with the member desk beside it on desktop. It should not be discarded simply because the new concepts have stronger titles. Its [member phone top](../../homepage-redesign/review/member-phone390-top.jpg) gets the current pair/court and desk action into a coherent early sequence. The advances explore different improvements: 09 improves the reference density and numeric relationships; 16 uses audience-specific hierarchy; 18 integrates early mobile actions and a distinctive functional scene. All three should keep #271's useful readable pair, explicit game route and complete roster access. A longer title, a smaller portrait or a shorter full-page image is not itself proof of an improvement.
